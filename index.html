01 / Project overview

Research background

The study looks beyond clinical treatment and considers the role of communication in supporting patients’ psychological resilience.

Research context

Patients undergoing chemotherapy may face physical and psychological challenges. The research examines therapeutic communication grounded in local cultural values as a contextual approach to patient care.

Research objective

To examine the influence of Silih Asah, Silih Asih, and Silih Asuh in nurse communication on resilience levels among lung cancer patients undergoing chemotherapy.

02 / Conceptual framework

Understanding Trisilas

The three values frame different dimensions of therapeutic interaction.

01 · AFFECTIVE

Silih Asih

Mutual care, empathy, and compassion. In nursing communication, this includes listening attentively, showing concern, and offering emotional support.

02 · COGNITIVE

Silih Asah

Mutual learning and knowledge-building. This is reflected in clear, educational communication that helps patients understand treatment and their experiences.

03 · SUPPORTIVE

Silih Asuh

Mutual guidance, care, and protection. In practice, this includes attention to patients’ comfort, safety, and practical needs.

03 / Research methodology

Study design & data

A one-group pretest–posttest study, with resilience measured before and after the Trisilas-based therapeutic communication intervention.

Research designOne-Group Pretest–Posttest Design
Participants20 lung cancer patients selected through total sampling based on the study’s inclusion criteria
Age range31–65 years
InstrumentModified Connor-Davidson Resilience Scale (CD-RISC), 48 Likert-scale items with four response options
Predictor variablesSilih Asah, Silih Asih, and Silih Asuh
Response variableResilience level (ordinal)
Statistical methodBayesian ordinal logistic regression (cumulative logit model)
The article describes data collection through individual questionnaire completion and observation of nurses’ therapeutic communication across pre-interaction, orientation, working, and termination stages.

05 / Key findings

Empathy and learning showed the strongest associations

The published Bayesian ordinal regression results report positive estimates for all three dimensions, with different levels of evidence.

Silih Asih · Empathy

β = 1.15

95% interval: 0.36–2.07. The article identifies empathy as the dimension with the strongest estimated effect. It reports an approximately 3.16-fold higher tendency toward a higher resilience level.

Silih Asah · Mutual learning

β = 1.07

95% interval: 0.31–2.04. Educational communication was associated with an approximately 2.92-fold higher tendency toward a higher resilience level.

Silih Asuh · Guidance & protection

β = 0.55

95% interval: −0.26–1.50. The estimate was positive, but the article reports that this dimension was not statistically significant.

Overall interpretation

The findings highlight the importance of both affective support and educational communication. Silih Asuh remains part of holistic care, even though its estimated association was not statistically significant in this study.

How to read the results: β values and 95% intervals above are transcribed from the article’s Bayesian model tables. The 3.16× and 2.92× figures are the article’s reported interpretations. They describe relative odds/tendency toward a higher ordinal resilience level, not an absolute probability or a percentage-point increase.
